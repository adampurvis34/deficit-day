# Hidden Calories

The photo shows the food. It does not show the oil in the pan, the butter on the steak,
or the dressing already mixed through the salad. This file is about closing that gap.

**The size of the problem:** the average tracked day underestimates real intake by
400-600 kcal, and roughly half of that is cooking fat, condiments and drinks. Diners
underestimate restaurant meals by 20-40%; in one large study adults were off by ~175 kcal
per meal on average and a quarter of people were off by 500+.

Omitting hidden fat is a *guaranteed* underestimate. Including it with a wide confidence
band is an honest estimate. Always prefer the second.

---

## The checklist

Walk this every time. Each line is a question about something that will not be visible
as a distinct object in the picture.

- [ ] **Was it cooked in fat?** Seared, sautéed, pan-fried, stir-fried, roasted, "crispy"
- [ ] **Was fat added after cooking?** Butter on steak, oil drizzle, finishing oil, melted
      butter on vegetables or seafood
- [ ] **Is there dressing, sauce or glaze?** Including pre-tossed salads
- [ ] **Is it breaded or battered?** Breading absorbs a lot of oil
- [ ] **Is there cheese melted into it** where you can't see the quantity?
- [ ] **Is there a spread?** Mayo, butter, aioli on a sandwich or burger
- [ ] **Are there drinks?** Especially alcohol, juice, soda, sweetened coffee
- [ ] **Was anything eaten alongside but not photographed?** Bread basket, chips, a bite
      of something else, the kids' leftovers
- [ ] **Is the visible portion the whole portion?** Was a second helping taken?

## Cooking fat — how much actually lands on the food

Not all the oil in the pan ends up eaten. These are *absorbed/retained* amounts.

| Method | Home cooking | Restaurant |
|---|---|---|
| Non-stick, light spray | 0-1 g (0-9 kcal) | n/a |
| Sautéed vegetables | 7-14 g (60-125 kcal) | 20-40 g (180-360 kcal) |
| Pan-seared protein | 7-10 g (60-90 kcal) | 15-30 g (135-270 kcal) |
| Stir-fry (whole dish) | 14-20 g (125-180 kcal) | 30-60 g (270-540 kcal) |
| Roasted vegetables | 10-15 g (90-135 kcal) | 20-30 g (180-270 kcal) |
| Scrambled eggs / omelette | 5-10 g (45-90 kcal) | 10-20 g (90-180 kcal) |
| Grilled (brushed) | 3-7 g (27-63 kcal) | 10-15 g (90-135 kcal) |
| Butter-basted steak | 10-15 g (90-135 kcal) | 15-25 g (135-225 kcal) |
| Deep-fried, breaded | — | absorbs 8-12% of food weight in oil |
| Deep-fried, unbreaded (fries) | — | absorbs 5-8% of food weight |

**Restaurants use 2-4 tablespoons of fat per dish** where a home cook uses one. Fat is
cheap and makes food sell. This is the single biggest reason restaurant estimates come
in low.

Quick conversions: 1 tbsp oil = 13.5 g = **119 kcal**. 1 tbsp butter = 14.2 g = **102 kcal**.
1 tsp oil = 4.5 g = **40 kcal**.

## Dressings and sauces

A "small" amount of dressing is usually 2-4 tbsp, not 1.

| Item | Typical real amount | kcal |
|---|---|---|
| Side salad, lightly dressed | 30 g (2 tbsp) | 90-145 |
| Entrée salad, tossed | 55-85 g (4-6 tbsp) | 165-410 |
| Restaurant dressing cup, if emptied | 55-60 g | 165-290 |
| Caesar on a large salad | 60-80 g | 290-385 |
| Mayo on a sandwich | 14-28 g | 95-190 |
| Aioli / special sauce on a burger | 20-30 g | 140-205 |
| Ranch for dipping | 40-60 g | 170-260 |
| Alfredo on a pasta entrée | 150-250 g | 315-525 |
| Marinara on a pasta entrée | 150-250 g | 90-150 |
| Butter on toast/bread | 7-14 g | 50-100 |
| Cream cheese on a bagel | 30-45 g | 105-160 |
| Sweetened coffee syrup | 2-4 pumps | 40-80 |

Ask whether dressing was on the side and whether it was all used. On an entrée salad
this one question can move the total by 250 kcal.

## Restaurant adjustment

When working from a *photo* of restaurant food with no published nutrition data, apply a
systematic upward adjustment to the fat-bearing components — not to the whole meal.

| Setting | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Chain with published data | Use their data. No adjustment. |
| Fast casual (Chipotle, Sweetgreen, Cava) | +5-10% — portions are scooped and fairly consistent |
| Casual sit-down (Cheesecake, Applebee's) | +20-30% |
| Independent / gastropub | +15-25% |
| Fine dining | +20-35% (butter-heavy technique) |
| Home cooked by Adam | No adjustment — but still count the oil |

This adjustment is why the calculator's uncertainty range is **asymmetric**. The error
distribution is skewed toward underestimating, so the band should be too: roughly
−10% / +25% for an un-adjusted restaurant dish.

## What *not* to over-correct

Do not double-count. If the visible sheen on roasted vegetables is already reflected in a
"roasted vegetables" density that includes oil, don't add oil again. Prefer building from
plain-food densities plus an explicit fat line — it keeps the accounting visible and
auditable.

And do not inflate everything reflexively. Grilled chicken with steamed vegetables is a
genuinely low-calorie plate. The correction belongs on dishes where fat is plausibly
hidden, not as a blanket tax.
